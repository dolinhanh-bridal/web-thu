The Bride’sDRESS GUIDE

8 things every bride should know before deciding on her wedding dress. Read it before your first fitting.

Dolinhanh

To the bride
Never choose a wedding dress just because “it’s beautiful”.

A dress can look beautiful in a photograph, on a mannequin, or on another bride. What matters is this: is it truly beautiful on you?

After years spent in the fitting room, we have learnt that most brides do not choose wrongly for want of beautiful dresses. They choose wrongly because they walk into the fitting without knowing what they are looking for, where to look, or what to ask before leaving a deposit.

This little book gathers exactly those things — eight sections, fifteen minutes to read, useful wherever you go to try dresses on.

Know yourself

Know your taste. Know your body. Know what makes you look your best. Before choosing a wedding dress, take a little time to understand the person who will wear it — you.

01 — Your taste
“Which kind of beautiful do I actually love?” Gather 5–10 photographs of dresses you love into one album, then look for what repeats — all full skirts, all lace, all off-shoulder? Look at your everyday wardrobe too: are you minimal or romantic, covered or sensual. The quiz overleaf will help you name your taste.
02 — Your body
“What are my proportions?” Stand in front of the mirror and compare three places: shoulders — waist — hips. No need to conclude yet — section 04 has a table of which figure suits which silhouette.
03 — Your best features
“What do I want to show, and what do I want to balance?” This is the what to show — what to soften question your stylist will certainly ask at the fitting.

Four kinds of bride we meet

Which way do you lean? Once you can name your taste, you will know which rail to start from when you walk into the fitting room.

Elegant minimal
Plain satin, a sheath or A-line with few details — the beauty is in the cut.
Splendid princess
A full ballgown skirt, sparkling beadwork, a long veil behind.
Dreamy romantic
Tulle and layers of organza light as cloud, fine sheer sleeves.
Modern sensual
A mermaid worn close, sheer lace, a low open back.
Not being able to name your style is perfectly fine A great many brides only know what they want once they have tried a few dresses on. Bring the album you have gathered — your stylist will find what they have in common for you. That is what a bridal house is for.

What to prepare before the fitting

Prepare properly and one fitting will be worth three rushed ones.

  • Wear a strapless bra or a nude bodysuit, and briefs with no visible edge. Bust padding is usually already sewn into the dresses.
  • Bring your wedding shoes or a pair of the same height so the length can be judged properly.
  • Hair pinned or tied up, close to the way you plan to wear it on the day.
  • Light make-up, avoid a deep lipstick — lipstick is what marks dresses most.
  • Take off most jewellery and your watch, so the whole picture stays clean.
  • Look through the catalogue first and choose about 5 dresses you like, then send the list ahead so they are hanging ready when you arrive.
  • Come with one or two people who genuinely understand your taste. A large group usually leaves you more undecided.
  • Come once your wedding date is set — only then can the dress be held and the alterations timed properly.
  • Know the venue in advance: indoors or outdoors, a high stage or low, whether you will be moving about a lot.
  • Have an answer ready for the most important question you will be asked: what would you like to show, and what would you rather soften?
How long a proper fitting takes About an hour and a half, and it should be private — one fitting room, one consultant with you throughout. If you have to wait, or share a mirror with other clients, the appointment will never be quiet enough for you to hear how you feel.

Underwear for a fitting

The question brides message us about most before a first fitting. The general rule: nude, no straps, no heavy underwire, no visible edges.

Better avoided

  • A bra with straps, or a back strapMost wedding dresses are off-shoulder or open-backed — straps show and you cannot see the true shape.
  • A white braWhite shows through fine tulle even more than nude does.
  • Heavy underwire, thick paddingIt changes the shape of the bust, so a dress made to those measurements will sit wrongly.
  • Thick lace briefs, low-riseThey press a line across the satin that shows clearly in photographs.
Wearing nothing is fine too Most wedding dresses already have a supportive corset and bust padding sewn inside. Many brides wear only nude briefs — do say so to your consultant, there is nothing to feel awkward about.

Three pairs worth bringing

The length of a dress is set to your height in your shoes. In flats the alterations will be wrong — in heels on the day the dress sits short or sweeps the floor. These three pairs cover everything.

Four things nobody warns you about

  • Put the dress and shoes on, then stand beside your groom and look in the mirror together: are the skirt volume and the length balanced side by side? Too much volume leaves the groom lost in photographs.
  • Wear the shoes at home a few evenings before the wedding. New shoes are stiff at the heel, and a full day in them will blister.
  • Put heel pads in beforehand, and carry plasters. The rubbing usually starts after two hours of standing.
  • If you have not bought your wedding shoes yet, bring a pair of the same height to the fitting. What matters is the height you will wear on the day.
What heel height is right Most brides choose 5 to 7 centimetres. Higher flatters the figure but aches after two hours of photographs; lower and the dress sweeps the ground. Outdoors or on grass, wear flatforms.

How many dresses to try

Try too few and you have nothing to compare. Try too many and you will not remember the first one.

  • Try all four basic silhouettes before settling on a direction: A-line, ballgown, mermaid and sheath.
  • Five is a good number. Past ten it all blurs and tires you.
  • Try at least one dress you are sure will not suit you — that is the one that surprises brides most.
  • In every dress, sit down, raise your arms, walk a few steps, not just stand still in front of the mirror.
  • Write one line about how it felt straight after each dress, before you try the next.
  • Ask your stylist for a short video of the front and the back of every dress. The back is the part you cannot see yourself, and the part guests see most.
  • Once you have tried them all, keep exactly three and try those again to decide.

How a fitting is arranged

Into the dress→veil on→shape adjusted→first look & photographs→photographs edited→sent back to you
Ask for photographs, don’t rely on the mirror A mirror flatters you; a photograph does not. For every dress, ask the studio to film the moment you first see yourself and to take a few frames. Look at them again that evening and you will see things you never noticed standing in the fitting room.

Silhouettes that suit your figure

We choose by proportion , never by weight. First, look in the mirror and compare three places: shoulders — waist — hips — whichever is widest tells you your shape. No shape is better than another; there are only silhouettes that suit it better.

If you hesitate between two shapes Choose the one that includes the part you would rather soften — a dress chosen that way is the safer bet. And at the fitting, your consultant will see it for you in thirty seconds.

A quick table for each concern

Now that you know your shape, look up what you would like handled gracefully — the next page draws each silhouette.

Fuller arms
Light puff sleeves, sheer tulle sleeves, an off-shoulder V — the shoulder line is drawn outwards, so the whole looks slimmer.
Smaller bust
A padded straight neckline, ruching at the bust, a square neck.
Fuller bust
A V neckline, wide straps that carry the shoulders.
Fuller hips
An A-line flaring from the waist. If you love a mermaid, choose one that flares from mid-thigh rather than the knee.
Petite figure
A high waist, a plain minimal skirt rather than layers of ruffles, a soft slit, moderate volume.
Marks or scars on the back
A sheer tulle back, lace across the back, or wide crossed straps.
Broad shoulders
A deep V, a soft off-shoulder, and avoid wide puff sleeves.
An undefined waist
An A-line or empire, gathered under the bust and falling softly. A narrow belt adds a point of interest; avoid stiff fabrics worn close.
A long body and shorter legs
Choose a high waist (empire) that lifts the gathering point — visually it lengthens the legs. Avoid a dropped waist.
Please remember this You do not need to lose weight, lighten your skin or change anything about yourself before walking into a fitting room. Finding the dress that suits you is our work.

Which lining suits your skin

The same lace dress gives four feelings on four skin tones. No lining is wrong — only untried against your skin. Where Dolinhanh begins:

Reading your own skin tone in ten seconds In natural light, turn your wrist and look at the veins. If they read blue or purple — a cool tone, and White and Blush Pink will look very clear. If they read green, slightly olive — a warm tone, and Nude is the truest match. If you see both, you are neutral and every shade works.

Three things to remember when choosing the lining

  • Look at it in the light of the party itself. The warm light of a restaurant and the white light of a fitting room give different results.
  • Photograph it, don’t only use the mirror. Two shades side by side in the same light — a photograph shows the difference better than a mirror.
  • Try it wearing the make-up you plan to have. Foundation usually reads a shade lighter than bare skin — enough to change the feeling of the whole dress.

Details on the dress to look at closely

Some things you never notice standing still in a mirror, yet feel very clearly after eight hours in the dress.

  • The corset line — does it sit on your true waist, or is it riding above it.
  • The cups — do they stay in place when you bend forward and raise your arms.
  • The skirt — how heavy is it; will you be holding it up the whole time you walk.
  • The train — how long is it, and is there a loop to gather it up for the reception.
  • The neckline and shoulders — do they press or leave red marks after fifteen minutes.
  • The beadwork — does it scratch your arms, or catch on the veil or your hair.
  • The lining — is it dense enough when a flash fires from behind you.
  • Zip and covered buttons: can you dress yourself, or will a second person always be needed.
  • Whatever needs altering — write it down during the fitting, not once you are home.
Ask outright while you are there If you would like an added overskirt, an off-shoulder detail, gloves or a different veil, ask the price during the fitting. These are the easiest costs to run into, and the easiest to avoid — you only have to ask first.

Fabric — four names worth knowing

Fabric decides how a dress falls, holds and moves on your body. Knowing these four names is enough to talk to any bridal house.

Five things to check while it is on you

  • Look at the dress under three kinds of light: the fitting-room lights, daylight from a window, and your phone’s flash.
  • Sit down for two minutes and stand up again — does the fabric crease and hold the crease .
  • Stand still for fifteen minutes — does it feel hot, itchy or airless .
  • Feel the inside of the dress: is the layer against your skin rough.
  • Walk a few steps and turn — does the dress move with you, or are you dragging it along.

We choose not just the finest fabrics, but the ones that tell your story

Questions to ask before you leave a deposit

Ask everything before you pay, and there will be nothing to argue about later. A decent studio will answer each one straight.

  • Is this dress still free on my exact wedding date ?
  • Once I have paid, can I change to another dress, and at what point is it locked?
  • If I gain or lose weight between now and the wedding, how will the dress be altered?
  • Alterations to my measurements — how are they charged, and how far can they go?
  • How many days do I keep the dress, counted from when, and what happens if I return it late?
  • What accessories come with the dress, and how are an extra veil or a tiara charged?
  • If the dress is stained or torn, when do I have to pay for it and what proportion?
  • How long before the wedding do I come for a final fitting?
  • On collection day, who will fit the dress on me?
  • Are there photographs or videos of the fitting so I can check the dress when I collect it?
  • If the wedding date moves, what happens to the deposit and the booking?

Not choosing a dress on a passing feeling

Feelings run high in a fitting room, and they are not always right. These seven lines will help you check calmly.

  • Don’t settle on the first dress that moves you. Set it aside and keep trying.
  • Sleep on it and look at the photographs the next day, in daylight.
  • Put the photographs of your final three side by side, cover the faces and look only at the shape.
  • Ask yourself: could I wear this for eight hours ?
  • Ask yourself: am I choosing it because I love it, or because the person with me does?
  • Ask yourself: looking at the photographs ten years from now , will I still see myself?
  • Still torn between two dresses: choose the one you keep describing to other people.
How you know you have found the dress You stop asking everyone around you “does it look good?”. You look in the mirror and you already know the answer.

In summary

Choosing a dress in five steps

The whole book comes down to five steps. Take them in order and your fitting will be half as long, and you will still choose the right dress.

01
Read your figure

Stand in front of the mirror and compare three places: shoulders — waist — hips. Whichever is widest tells you your shape.

02
Choose the silhouette

Every figure has one or two safe silhouettes. Begin the fitting there, then try one that goes against it, just to know.

03
Try the fabric

In the same silhouette, changing the fabric changes everything. Lace, tulle, satin and organza give you four different women.

04
Settle the lining colour

The step almost nobody thinks about: the lining decides how the dress reads in photographs. Choose it for your skin.

05
Choose the veil

A veil changes the whole picture, yet it is usually left to the last minute. A floor-sweeping veil for a formal ceremony, an elbow-length one for outdoors, a two-tier veil with a blusher for the family ceremony and for church. Try veils at the same appointment as the dress, and look in the mirror with it off as well.
